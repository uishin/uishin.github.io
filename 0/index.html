Project 1: The Selfie — Wrong Way vs. Right Way

This part demonstrates how camera-to-subject distance affects perspective distortion in portraits, independent of how large the face appears in the final frame.

The Two Photos

Wrong way: camera ~10 inches from face, no zoom.

Right way: camera ~8 feet away, zoomed in to match face size.

Why This Happens

Under perspective projection, a point's apparent size scales with f / Z, where f is focal length and Z is its distance from the camera. Facial features are not all at the same depth — the nose sits several centimeters closer to the camera than the ears or cheeks.

When the camera is very close, that few-centimeter depth difference is a large fraction of the total distance, so near features (the nose) get magnified much more than far features (the ears), producing the bulging, distorted look of a typical close-up selfie.

When the camera is far away, that same depth difference is a tiny fraction of the total distance, so all parts of the face are magnified almost equally — the projection approaches orthographic, and proportions look natural. Zooming in from far away just increases f to bring the face back to a comparable size in the frame, without reintroducing the depth-dependent distortion, since it's distance (not focal length) that controls the relative scaling between near and far features.
